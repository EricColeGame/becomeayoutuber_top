If you searched for "Become a Youtuber," you may have expected a tutorial about starting a real channel. This article is about something narrower and more specific: the Roblox experience **be a youtuber** (place ID 107137293512986, universe 7275724106), a creator-simulation game by the Roblox user Majicos_official. You can open it directly at [the official Roblox page for be a youtuber](https://www.roblox.com/games/107137293512986/be-a-youtuber).

It is worth clearing up three mix-ups before going further. This is not *Youtubers Life*, the Steam and console game. It is not the separate Roblox experience titled "Be a YouTuber!" with different punctuation. And it is not a real-world course about building a YouTube channel. ## What the Official Page Actually Establishes

The official Roblox record, retrieved October 14, 2026, gives a small set of hard facts. The experience was created on February 21, 2025, by Majicos_official. Servers hold up to 50 players. The record lists roughly 119,000 visits. That is a modest footprint compared with blockbuster Roblox titles, which matters for how you set expectations: you are joining a small-to-mid-sized experience, not a giant.

The official description frames the game as a career climb. You begin as a small creator with basic gear. From there you record videos, upload content, and gain subscribers and views. Videos earn in-game money. That money goes into upgrades: microphones, cameras, gaming PCs and streaming studios. The advertised endpoint is becoming the top YouTuber on the server, with a stated goal of reaching one million subscribers.

Those are the advertised features. They are broad descriptions, not a manual.

## The Advertised Loop, Step by Step

Stripped to its bones, the loop the developer advertises runs in four beats: record, upload, grow, upgrade. You start under-equipped on purpose, so the early game is about producing content with basic tools. Subscribers and views accumulate from what you upload. Money follows from the videos. Upgrades then feed back into the next recording session, which should in theory produce better results.

## Where Real-World YouTube Thinking Fits

Some real-world creator instincts transfer usefully here, as long as you treat them as general reasoning rather than documented mechanics. Consistency is the obvious one: a game about accumulating views and subscribers rewards repeated sessions more than a single long one. Deliberate spending is another: because money comes from videos and flows into upgrades, rushing the first upgrade you can afford may leave you short later.

The game does not document whether upload frequency, video "quality," or upgrade order actually changes your subscriber curve. Nobody outside the developer can tell you the formula, and the official page does not publish one. So the sensible approach is to observe your own numbers after each session and let your results, not a guide, tell you what worked.

There is also a temptation to import real-world advice wholesale — niches, thumbnails, sponsorships, AdSense. None of that is documented as part of this experience. Where the game touches those ideas at all, it does so as simplified in-game abstractions, and the official description names only microphones, cameras, gaming PCs and streaming studios.

## What Is Not Documented

Several things people commonly search for simply have no official support:

- **Promo codes.** The official page documents no code system, no redemption box, and no free-cash codes. If a site claims otherwise, it is guessing or worse.
- **Prices and item names.** No upgrade costs or specific equipment names appear in the official description.
- **Studio tiers.** The description mentions streaming studios as an upgrade category but does not enumerate tiers.
- **Subscriber milestones.** Only the one-million goal is advertised. Nothing between your first subscriber and that number is documented.
- **Controls.** No keybinds or input scheme are published.

The reliable way to fill these gaps is to read the official description yourself on the [Roblox experience page](https://www.roblox.com/games/107137293512986/be-a-youtuber), play a session and read the in-game UI, and check Majicos_official's Roblox profile and any community group the creator maintains for announcements. If the developer adds a code system later, that is where it would surface first.

## A Sensible First Session

Join a server, expect up to 49 other players competing for the same leaderboard, and spend your first few minutes reading every prompt the game shows you rather than clicking through. Locate the recording and uploading interaction before you worry about upgrades. Watch what your first videos return in views, subscribers and money, then compare that against the prices the in-game shop displays. Buy the upgrade that removes your most obvious limitation, not the one that looks most impressive.

Keep a rough note of what each session earned. Because the developer publishes no economy figures, your own log is the only reliable baseline you will have — and it will be more accurate for your playstyle than any generic advice.

## FAQ

**Is Become a Youtuber the same as Youtubers Life?**
No. Youtubers Life is a separate commercial game on PC and consoles. This is a Roblox experience created by Majicos_official.

**Are there codes or free-cash codes for this experience?**
None are documented. The official Roblox page describes no code system or redemption feature, so any code list you find is unverified. Check the official page and the creator's community for announcements instead.

**How many subscribers do I need to win?**
The official description advertises a one-million-subscriber goal and a "top YouTuber on the server" position. No intermediate milestones or ranking formula are published.

**How many people play at once?**
The official record lists a server size of up to 50 players.

**Can anyone guarantee I will hit one million subscribers?**
No. The goal is advertised as the game's endpoint, not as an outcome any guide can promise. Progress depends on the game's own systems, which the developer has not fully documented.
