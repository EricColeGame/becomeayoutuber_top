## What the Roblox Experience "be a youtuber" Actually Is

The verified record on the [official Roblox page for be a youtuber](https://www.roblox.com/games/107137293512986/be-a-youtuber) identifies it as place ID 107137293512986, universe 7275724106, created by the Roblox user **Majicos_official**. The place was created on **2025-02-21**, supports servers of **up to 50 players**, and had roughly **119,000 visits** when that record was retrieved on **2026-10-14**.

It is a creator-simulation experience. You begin as a small creator with basic gear, record and upload videos, accumulate subscribers and views, earn in-game money, and spend that money on better equipment. The advertised endpoint is becoming the top YouTuber on the server, with a stated goal of one million subscribers.

That description matters because three different things share almost the same name, and mixing them up leads to bad advice:

| What people mean | What it actually is |

| This article's subject | A free Roblox experience by Majicos_official |
| *Youtubers Life* | A separate commercial game sold on PC and consoles, unrelated to Roblox |
| "Be a YouTuber!" | A different Roblox experience with a similar title |

If a guide is talking about buying a camera in real life, it is not describing this game.

## The Advertised Loop: Record, Upload, Grow, Earn

The official description outlines a cycle rather than a single goal. Stripped to what is actually stated, it goes like this:

1. Start as a small creator with basic gear.
2. Record videos.
3. Upload content.
4. Gain subscribers and views.
5. Earn money from videos.
6. Spend money on upgrades.
7. Climb toward being the top YouTuber on the server, with one million subscribers as the advertised target.

What the description does **not** do is explain the order in which the game forces these steps, how long a "video" takes to produce, or how the recording action is triggered. Those are in-game details, not published documentation. A new player should read the on-screen tutorial and interface labels during their first session rather than relying on a guide that fills in those blanks with invented hotkeys.

One useful framing: the subscribers, views and money in this experience are game systems. They are numbers the experience tracks for you. They are not connected to a real YouTube account, and nothing you do in the game changes a real channel's analytics.

## Practical Approach for a New Player

Treat your first session as reconnaissance rather than a race. Load in, read every prompt the game gives you, and note which pieces of starter gear you are handed — that tells you what the game considers your baseline before any upgrades. Then run the loop once end to end: record, upload, and watch what changes in your subscriber and view counters and in your money balance. That single cycle teaches you more about the economy than any outside guide can, because the game will show you its own numbers.

From there, decide deliberately whether you want to spread spending across all four advertised upgrade categories or push one category first. The description lists microphones, cameras, PCs and studios without ranking them, so there is no documented "correct" order. Experiment, and keep in mind that progress is not guaranteed to be fast — the one-million-subscriber target is advertised as an ambition, not a promise, and your results depend on how you play.
