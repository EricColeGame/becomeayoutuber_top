If you searched for "Become a Youtuber" and landed on a Roblox page instead of a video-editing tutorial, you are in the right place. The experience **be a youtuber** (place 107137293512986, universe 7275724106) is a creator-simulation game built by the Roblox user **Majicos_official**. You begin as a nobody with basic gear, record and upload videos, collect subscribers and views, earn in-game money, and spend that money on better equipment. The advertised finish line is one million subscribers.

A quick identity check matters here, because several things share similar names. This article is about the Roblox experience created by Majicos_official, not the Steam and console title *Youtubers Life*, not the separate Roblox experience *Be a YouTuber!*, and not real-world advice about running an actual YouTube channel.

## What the Official Roblox Page Documents

The official game page is the only authority on what the game is and what it advertises. According to the record retrieved on October 14, 2026, the place was created on February 21, 2025, supports servers of up to 50 players, and had about 119,000 visits at that time.

The description advertises a specific progression fantasy:

- Start as a small creator with basic gear.
- Record videos.
- Gain subscribers and views.
- Earn money from your videos.
- Upload content.
- Upgrade microphones, cameras, gaming PCs, and streaming studios.
- Become the top YouTuber on the server.
- Reach a one-million-subscriber goal.

That is the full advertised scope. Everything else — prices, item names, tier lists, subscriber thresholds, and controls — is not documented on the official page, which is why you should treat any confident numbers you see elsewhere with suspicion. You can read the source yourself on the [official be a youtuber page on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/107137293512986/be-a-youtuber).

## Why Upgrades Matter More Than Any Single Video

The advertised structure implies a compounding system. You record with basic gear, earn money from what you upload, then convert that money into better tools. Better tools should make the recording side of the loop smoother, which supports more or better output, which supports more subscribers and views, which supports more money.

That is reasoning from the advertised features, not a guaranteed formula. The page does not state how much any upgrade improves your results, whether upgrades gate certain content types, or whether income scales with subscribers, views, or something else entirely. What you can say with confidence is that the game deliberately starts you under-equipped, so the intended answer to "I am not growing fast enough" is almost certainly "keep the loop running and reinvest."

A practical consequence: do not hoard in-game cash waiting for a perfect purchase you cannot yet see. Equally, do not assume the most expensive thing in a shop is always the right next step. Check what each upgrade actually changes in the in-game interface before buying.

## The One-Million Goal and the Server Ranking

Two separate targets are advertised, and players often blur them together.

The **one-million-subscriber goal** is a personal milestone — a long-term objective attached to your own channel's growth.

**Becoming the top YouTuber on the server** is a comparative position. Servers hold up to 50 players, so this framing is about outperforming the other creators sharing your session, not about topping a global leaderboard. The official description does not explain how that ranking is measured, whether it resets between sessions, or whether it tracks subscribers, views, or earnings. If the ranking matters to you, watch the in-game leaderboard or scoreboard and see what moves when you upload.

## A Practical Way to Approach Your First Sessions

Treat your first sessions as data collection rather than a race. Learn the recording and uploading steps until they are automatic. Note what your first videos pay out so you understand your own baseline income. Then buy the upgrade that removes your most obvious bottleneck — if recording feels slow or limited, that is your signal.

Because the game is a 50-player social experience, the server you land on shapes your experience. Some sessions will have creators far ahead of you, which makes the "top YouTuber" framing feel distant. That is normal. Your comparison target is your own previous session, not the player who has been grinding for weeks.

Finally, no guide can promise you a fast climb to one million subscribers. The advertised goal is described as a goal, not a shortcut, and the actual pace depends on mechanics the official page does not publish.

## FAQ

### Is "be a youtuber" the same as Youtubers Life?

No. *Youtubers Life* is a separate commercial game on Steam and consoles. This article covers the Roblox experience created by Majicos_official.

### Are there promo codes or free-cash codes?

None are documented. The official page and the creator's description do not mention a code system. Check the official game page and the creator's Roblox community group for any future announcements.

### How do I reach one million subscribers?

The official description advertises the goal but not the underlying numbers. The advertised path is to record, upload, earn, upgrade your microphone, camera, gaming PC, and streaming studio, and repeat. Treat any specific subscriber threshold you read elsewhere as unverified.

### How many players fit in one server?

Up to 50, according to the official record. That makes the "top YouTuber on the server" objective a session-level competition rather than a global one.

### What are the controls?

Not documented on the official page. Learn them from the in-game tutorial or the settings menu, since the description publishes no control scheme.
