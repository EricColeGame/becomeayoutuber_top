The Roblox experience **be a youtuber** is a creator-simulation game made by the Roblox user Majicos_official. If you searched "Become a Youtuber" expecting a guide to launching a real channel, this is a different thing entirely: it is a game about *pretending* to be a creator, where progress is tracked in in-game subscribers, views, and money rather than ad revenue or sponsorships.

It is also worth clearing up three lookalikes. This is not *Youtubers Life*, the Steam and console game. It is not the separate Roblox experience *Be a YouTuber!*. And it is not a course or tutorial about real-world YouTube. The [official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/107137293512986/be-a-youtuber) is the authority here, and its description is short — so a lot of what circulates online about the game is guesswork. ## What the Game Actually Is

According to the official listing, the experience sits at place ID 107137293512986 (universe 7275724106), was created on February 21, 2025, and supports servers of up to 50 players. As of October 14, 2026, the page showed roughly 119,000 visits.

The premise described there is a rags-to-riches creator story. You begin as a small creator with basic gear, record videos, gain subscribers and views, earn money from your videos, upload content, and upgrade your equipment. The advertised end goal is reaching one million subscribers and becoming the top YouTuber on your server.

That is the entire official pitch. Everything else — prices, item names, tier lists, control schemes, income math — is either something you observe inside the game or something someone made up.

## How Real-World "Become a YouTuber" Habits Map Onto the Game

Because the game simulates creator life, some real-world instincts translate conceptually — and only conceptually. Consistency matters in both: publishing regularly is the documented way to gain subscribers and views in the game, just as a real channel grows through repeated uploads. Upgrading your gear before chasing bigger numbers is another parallel the description supports.

What does not translate is the specifics. Real-world tactics like thumbnail testing, search optimization, sponsorship deals, and ad revenue have no documented counterpart in this experience. The game's version of "what works" is whatever its own systems reward, and those systems are not published. If you enjoy the fantasy of building a creator career, the loop will make sense to you; if you want a checklist of real-world channel advice, this game is not that.
